# Slide plan — before the Level 4 demo

**Purpose:** name the ceiling Level 3 hit, explain why the task had to change, and set the
room up to watch an agent produce decisions and refuse to make them.
**Length:** 5 slides, ~3 minutes.
**Source evidence:** [../RUN-SHEET.md](../RUN-SHEET.md), [../FINDINGS.md](../FINDINGS.md),
[../../LADDER.md](../../LADDER.md).

**Design direction:** ladder motif, rung 4 lit. Carry Level 3's closing line across as the
visual thread — this deck answers it rather than starting a new chapter. Nothing here
reveals what the run produced; the after-deck lands all of that.

---

## Slide 1 — Level 4: Orchestrating

**Takeaway:** The harness can say no. Something still has to say what to do.

- Level 3 bounds what **may** happen. It has no opinion about what **should**.
- Level 4 puts a pipeline in front of the work — and a person inside it.

**Visual:** ladder, rung 4 lit, with the Level 3 closing line carried over from the
previous deck's last slide.

**Speaker note:** Ten seconds. The room heard the ceiling ninety seconds ago; do not
re-argue it.

---

## Slide 2 — The task had to change

**Takeaway:** The small task had no decisions in it.

- Levels 1–3: *add an email address to owners.* Chosen because it was decision-free —
  which is what made it a fair output comparison, and what makes it useless here.
- Level 4: **an owner books a visit against a vet's published availability.**
- Today `Visit` carries a pet, a bare `LocalDate` and a description. No vet, no time of
  day. **It cannot be added as a column.**

**Visual:** the two task sentences, one above the other, the old one greyed.

**Speaker note:** Thirty seconds, and say it before anything else runs. If the room
decides the task was swapped to flatter the pipeline, the whole segment reads as a sales
pitch.

---

## Slide 3 — Four gates, one rule

**Takeaway:** The agent produces the decisions. It does not make them.

| Hard gate | What the agent produces | What a human does |
|---|---|---|
| Slice adjudication | five slices, every choice left `pending` | disposes each one |
| Objection adjudication | objections, each grounded in quoted spec text | adjudicates |
| Plan approval | the spec, the plan, and the counts still outstanding | approves, or sends it back |
| Integration approval | objections against the built code | disposes, before anything merges |

- Two **soft** gates sit alongside them — the choice stories and the consultation
  voices. They report a count and let the work past.
- The rule the level exists to demonstrate: **no agent writes a disposition or an
  adjudication, in any state, ever.**

**Visual:** four gates in a line, each with a gap in it that only a person fills.

---

## Slide 4 — `pending` is the mechanism

**Takeaway:** A word in a file is the whole gate.

- Hard gates **block** on `pending`. Soft gates **report a count**.
- Nothing proceeds while a decision is unmade. That is the entire enforcement.

**Visual:** a slice record open at the disposition field, the word `pending` large.

**Speaker note:** This sets up the slow beat — one disposition, typed live, and let it
feel slow. Also warn the room now that we jump between checkpoint branches: the pipeline
does not fit in stage time, and pretending otherwise would be the one dishonest thing in
the talk.

---

## Slide 5 — What to watch for

**Takeaway:** Watch an agent produce decisions and refuse to make them.

1. Where does the human's attention **actually** go? (Watch the first gate.)
2. Does a governed pipeline buy any **exemption** from Level 3's harness?
3. Does anything here know **when to stop**?

**Visual:** three questions, the third emphasised.

**Speaker note:** Question 3 is what the segment is for. Do not answer it now. After ten
minutes of watching a pipeline work, the room will want to conclude that the pipeline is
the answer — question 3 is the thread that stops that, and it pays off at the end.
